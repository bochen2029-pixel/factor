## 1 · Mail lanes

| assumption (spec) | verdict | source | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| §4: "Mailbox over IMAP or Graph, read only" yields near-real-time frames. | **PARTIAL.** Only IMAP IDLE is sub-second. Graph and Gmail push are minute-grain. | [RFC 2177](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2177.html); [Graph change-notification latency table](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/change-notifications-overview) | Make world→frame lag a declared per-lane property in the lane contract header, not an assumption. §19's "frame arrival to verb p50 < 200 ms" is honest only because it starts at frame arrival; the account must print the *other* half. |

Graph supplies an opaque delta token and an etag — neither is ordered. | [RFC 7162](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7162); [Gmail push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push) | Require `CONDSTORE` on any IMAP lane (refuse the lane if the server lacks it). | Gmail push is a frame. | **PARTIAL.** The payload is `{emailAddress, historyId}` only — no content, so a second authenticated fetch is always required. `watch` expires in **7 days**; Google recommends renewing **daily**. Max **one notification per user per second**; excess is dropped. Google states notifications are not 100% reliable and a small percentage are dropped. | [Gmail push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push); [Google Calendar push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push) (same reliability language) | Push is an *accelerator*; the lane's correctness must come from `history.list` cursoring, which is also what makes F-CONSERVE provable. |
| Practical polling floor, Gmail API. | ~1 s is affordable. Per-user quota is **6,000 units/minute**; `history.list` costs **2**, `messages.list` 5, `messages.get` 20, `watch` 100. A 1 s history poll = 120 units/min, 2% of quota. | [Gmail API usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/quota) | Set the mail lane floor at 1–5 s on Gmail; the constraint is courtesy, not quota. |

| IMAP/Graph auth is a solved detail. | **FAILS.** Basic auth for POP/IMAP in Exchange Online is already gone; OAuth is mandatory. ## 3 · Calendar lanes

| assumption (spec) | verdict | source | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| §4: "Calendar over CalDAV, Graph, or ICS" gives near-real-time change frames. | **PARTIAL.** Two of the three are poll-only. | see rows below | Declare calendar as a **polled lane with a 30–60 s floor**, with push as an accelerator. |
| CalDAV supports push. | **FAILS in the base protocol.** CalDAV has no native push; change detection is polling a collection's CTag or a sync-token (RFC 6578). **WebDAV-Push** exists but is an Internet-Draft (`draft-bitfire-webdav-push`) layered on Web Push (RFC 8030) + VAPID (RFC 8292) + aes128gcm (RFC 8291), implemented by DAVx5 and Xandikos; the draft itself tells clients to keep polling, only less often. | [Push for WebDAV draft](https://bitfireat.github.io/webdav-push/draft-bitfire-webdav-push-00.html); [Xandikos](https://github.com/jelmer/xandikos) | Poll `sync-token` at 30–60 s. | Google Calendar push gives change frames. | **PARTIAL.** Channels expire (7 days in practice) with **no automatic renewal** — you must create a replacement channel with a new id. The receiving URL needs a valid, non-self-signed SSL certificate. | [Google Calendar push](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push) | Use `events.list` with `syncToken` as the truth path; push only shortens the wait. | ICS is a lane. | **PARTIAL.** An ICS URL is a whole-file fetch: no revision, no deletes except by diffing two snapshots, no push. | [Graph/CalDAV rows above, by contrast] | If ICS is kept, the producer must emit a synthetic revision per fetch and a computed delete set, and the extraction falsifier must include a "row vanished from the file" case. |

## 7 · The protocol the operator called "MCP 2.0 stateless"

| assumption (spec) | verdict | source | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| There is an "MCP 2.0". | **FAILS on the name; HOLDS on the substance.** MCP revisions are dated, not semver. ## Assumptions the spec should add

These are load-bearing facts the blueprint currently assumes silently. Each should become a stated assumption, a lane-contract field, or a falsifier.

1. **No inbound socket exists.** Every push mechanism named in §4 — Graph webhooks, Gmail Pub/Sub push, Google Calendar `watch`, QuickBooks, Xero — requires a publicly reachable HTTPS endpoint. A resident on a desktop has none. Either every lane pulls (Pub/Sub pull subscription, Azure Event Hubs, IMAP IDLE, CDC, sync-token) or the relay is a named, hash-pinned third party whose bytes appear on the egress ledger. State which, per lane.

2. **World→frame lag is a lane property with a number.** §19's budgets all start at frame arrival. Graph mail is up to 3 minutes; QuickBooks aggregates on the order of minutes; CalDAV and ICS are poll-floor-bound. An obligation's `horizon_ns` computed without the lane's lag is wrong by the lag.

3. **A monotonic source revision is the exception, not the rule.** §3 and §5 build the deposit clock and the stale-refusal law on `rev`. Only IMAP-with-CONDSTORE and Gmail `historyId` supply one. Graph, CalDAV, QuickBooks and Xero do not. Define the synthetic per-lane revision and forbid applying "a lower revision is refused" to an opaque token.

4. **Lane producers have sockets, therefore egress.** Law 10 counts the kernel's egress (zero) and the hand's (per row). A read-only mailbox lane is still an authenticated outbound connection carrying a credential, and a cloud browser's page fetches never touch the hand at all. Either the egress ledger covers producers and rented machines, or §10 says explicitly what it does not count.

5. **Provider events are metadata, not records.** Graph basic notifications, Gmail push, Google Calendar push, QuickBooks and Xero all deliver an identifier and require a second authenticated fetch. That fetch happens *before* the kernel judges, so it is not a LOOK and is not on the deep budget — but it costs, it can fail, and it can return a record that changed again in the interim.

6. **Credential and subscription lifetime is a first-class obligation class on lane `self`.** Google refresh tokens die at 7 days in Testing status and at 6 months of non-use; Gmail `watch` dies at 7 days; Graph subscriptions die at 7 days (1 day with resource data); Google Calendar channels do not auto-renew; Xero disables a webhook after 24 hours of failed delivery. Law 12 ranks `self` above every counterparty — these are the rows that justify it, and each has a hard date.

7. **A third-party receipt is a URL until FACTOR downloads it.** Orgo screenshots and Kernel replays live on vendor infrastructure. The tape must hold the hash of bytes FACTOR fetched and stored, never a vendor link.

8. **Cap custody is a separate clause from cap existence.** "A cap the machine cannot raise" requires that the credential the hand holds cannot write the cap. Add **F-CAP**: with the hand's own credential, attempt to raise the cap; the provider must refuse.

9. **Quantization is part of the serve pin.** The same weights served at a different quantization are a different judge.

10. **Providers withhold events while you are down.** QuickBooks may not deliver later events until an earlier one is acknowledged; Xero holds events for 31 days and replays them in order after re-enablement. F-CONSERVE's five outcomes need a sixth — *withheld-by-provider, later replayed* — and the planted lie is a replayed batch counted twice.

11. **Event coverage is partial and class-specific.** Xero has no webhook for payments or bank transactions, so `books.pay` outcomes are polled and their grades are read at a poll-bound horizon. A licence granted on a class whose outcomes arrive late is a licence granted on stale grades.

12. **Standing subscription cost has no line in the writ.** §9's budget passes price human minutes, deep-gear dollars and worker capacity. A cloud browser, an agent computer, an agent inbox, an Event Hubs namespace and a connector runtime are fixed monthly costs that no gate can hold back. Add a fixed-cost stanza so the Friday account can print the deputy's rent alongside its egress.
