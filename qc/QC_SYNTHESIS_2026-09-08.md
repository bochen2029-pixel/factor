## §2 · Where reviewers disagreed, and how it was resolved

- **BLAKE3 versus BLAKE2b.** O1 argued BLAKE3 with derive-key and keyed modes; B argued BLAKE2b for compatibility with `verify_chain.py`, nib, engram, connectome and scriptorium; O3 noted the blake3 package was absent on the box. Resolved for BLAKE2b-256: it has native keyed mode and personalization, so every construction O1 asked for is available, and the family's verifier keeps working. D15.
- **Who holds the tape key.** O1 wanted the tape key sealed to the hand so a compromised kernel cannot rewrite history; that puts a round trip on the hot path. Resolved by two witnesses: the kernel keys its chain, the hand records and signs the head at every effect, the console anchors daily. D29.
- **Grade as its own operation.** RECD and ULT keep Grade first-class; KCMP and the irreducible-operations file fold it into Fold. Resolved as writing, not count: the folds table carries a clock per reducer and the grades reducer is named with its exogenous input. B 3.7.
- **A separate licensor machine.** EMPTY wanted the license written by a process that cannot act on separate hardware. Resolved with option (a): the license fold is a deterministic reduction bounded by the monotone law, F-MONOTONE guards it, and `factor verify --license` re-derives it from the tape without the kernel. O4 #24.
- **Shadow versus canary at one rung.** A, B, O5 and RECON all found the contradiction; resolved by giving the canary its own rung. D3.
- **Widening on outcomes without a yes.** SEXOPS argued outcomes should widen scope directly; RECON and the blueprint keep the yes. Resolved by stating what both mean: a ratification is a rule with an outcome criterion, widening inside the rule is written by outcomes, only the rule needs a signature. O4 #23.
- **"Two cache lines."** O3 noted the hot walk touches both lines either way. Kept at 128 bytes for the digest and the map, with the note that the walk's fields can be moved onto line 0 if the readout budget is missed.
